# THE ROADS I MADE

**A short, dark, third-person action game.**
Story bible — v0.1

Working title alternatives: *All The Roads I Made* · *Carrion Pilgrim* · *The Guest* · *Nuhal*

---

## 0. The pitch

An old mapmaker drags a corpse across a dying country toward a mountain, to kill
the thing inside it. He has been doing this for twenty years. He is the only
person in the game who explains anything to you, he is right about almost every
fact, and he is the worst thing that has ever happened to this world.

Target length: **2.5–3 hours** for a first playthrough. Five endings, sequential,
roughly 5 hours total for everything.

---

## 1. Design pillars

1. **Weight over spectacle.** Animation carries the game. No combo strings, no
   flashy VFX, no upgrade tree. He is a seventy-year-old man with ruined hands
   and he fights like it, from the first minute to the last.
2. **Duration is the emotional weapon.** Long silent traversal, then one violent
   act, then silence again. Never rush the player through anything.
3. **No HUD.** No health bar, no objective marker, no map UI. Damage reads on the
   body and in the animation. He is a mapmaker; if you want to know where you
   are, he will tell you.
4. **The frame is the trick.** Everything shown is factually true. The narration
   is what lies. Act 3 removes the narration and replays the footage.
5. **Nothing is upstream-recoverable.** The catastrophe was locked in before the
   game began. No ending averts it. No ending is meant to feel earned.

---

## 2. The world

Bronze-age-adjacent. Bone, bronze, wool, tar, salt, standing stones. No plate
armor, no castles, no kings on screen. Old enough that a thing living under a
mountain is unremarkable — it is geography, not theology.

**The Sill** — the high dead country the whole game takes place in. Salt flats in
the south, river valleys in the middle, drowned lowlands, then the mountain.

**Nuhal** — the mountain. Visible from almost every exterior in the game, at
every distance, in every weather. It should never be absent from the skybox.

**The Guest** — what's inside. Never named by anyone who has seen it. Never
clearly shown. Never speaks in words.

### The engine (the upstream truth)

The Guest is waking, and it wakes in proportion to **how much of the living world
is oriented toward it.** Not worship — *attention*. Need pointed in its direction.

To generate that attention, it makes people sick. Slowly, over months,
indistinguishable from ordinary illness. Every death buys it a hundred grieving
minds and a few of those walk to the mountain. More sickness → more pilgrims →
more awake → more sickness. It has been running for four centuries. It is now
almost open.

**It only opens to grief.** A person carrying a weapon and no dead cannot find
the way in. A person carrying their dead can.

Consequences — every escape route the player will try to imagine:

- *Kill it.* You cannot. It does not register damage. It does not register you.
- *Don't go.* Thousands are walking. One man is a rounding error. The world ends
  on schedule without him.
- *Warn everyone.* Telling people about the mountain makes them think about the
  mountain. **The truth is also food.** This is why the resistance never explains
  itself, and lets the world hate it instead.
- *Bury the dead properly.* The grief still points at Nuhal.

There is no act of heroism, warning, or sacrifice available in this world that
isn't also fuel.

## 3. Cast

### SARN — the mapmaker (playable)

Seventy-one. Surveyor. Spent twenty years charting the Sill for exactly one
reason: to find a way into Nuhal and kill the Guest. Every pass, every ford,
every measured mile is a siege plan.

**The maps got used.** Other people followed his lines. He collapsed the journey
from two years to four months and made Nuhal reachable by anyone with a grudge
against death. He is personally responsible for the last, steepest acceleration
of the apocalypse. He knows. He considers it acceptable.

*He talks constantly* — to the corpse, in full conversation, supplying both
sides. After twenty years he has Kelem's half memorized. This is also the
player's only narration, only exposition, and only source of interpretation. He
is funny, bitter, precise, and almost always factually correct.

**The price he's already paid:** his hands. Twenty years of drafting and hauling
in cold. The fingers are wrecked. He cannot grip properly. Every fight in the
game is an old man who can barely hold his weapon, and this never improves.

### KELEM — the body

Sarn's friend of sixty years, dead eleven of them. Surveyed with him their whole
lives — the two of them alone in high country, year after year. Preserved,
wrapped, hauled across the continent on attempt after failed attempt.

Sarn has not buried him. He will not. He tells himself it is devotion.

**It is a ticket.** The mountain opens to grief. Sarn needs a corpse to get in.

Kelem was the one who kept saying someone should stop selling the charts. Sarn
laughed at him every time.

### VESSA — the masked woman at the mouth

The one who takes the mask off at 80%. Late thirties, dying, entirely calm. Has
known who Sarn is for two decades. Has watched him kill her people for two
decades. Asks him, once, to turn around.

### ITHE — the girl from Dresh

Joins at Leg 3, the village with more graves than houses. Lost everyone. Asks to
come. Sarn lets her, because a second grief is a second key and his own is
wearing thin after eleven years.

**She never judges him.** Not once, at any point. That's the rule. Judgment would
let him off — it would make it a debate he could win. Instead she is just kind to
him, repeatedly, and in Act 3 you find out she spent the entire journey trying to
talk to him and he talked over her every time.

He leaves her on the mountain.

### THE MASKED (the resistance)

Villagers from the Sill. The ones who buried everybody and stayed. They wear
masks because of the sickness on their faces, not because of any cult.

They burn shrines, because shrines draw pilgrims. They block passes, because the
roads kill people. They hang bodies along the roads as a warning and never
explain it, because explaining points people at Nuhal. They attack Sarn on sight
because he is *the man with the maps*, and they have known this for twenty years.

He has killed roughly two hundred of them.

They are correct about everything in this game. They have no victory available to
them and they know it. They are slowing something that cannot be stopped, because
the alternative is not slowing it.

## 4. The reveal (structural, not a twist)

**Nothing is hidden from the player. The context is.** Three frames do the work:

1. **His voice.** He is the only interpreter in the game. You side with him by
   default because you have no other source.
2. **Presentation of the enemy.** Masked, hooded, silent, armed with hooks and
   knives, burning shrines, stringing up bodies. They behave exactly like a death
   cult, Sarn calls them one, and he is not lying — he believes it.
3. **Distance.** The camera never gets close to anyone who isn't Sarn. Villagers
   are seen across fields. The masked are seen at combat range and then they are
   dead. You never see a face until Vessa's.

And the load-bearing one, seeded but not stated until Ending E:

**Sixty years ago it was Sarn who found Nuhal first, and he sent Kelem ahead into
unmapped ground, because that's what you do with the second man on a survey.**
Kelem took what was meant for him. Sarn watched him die over eleven months.

He is not avenging Kelem. He is carrying the evidence. The rage is what you build
when the alternative is one sentence you cannot say.

## 5. Structure

### ACT I–II — The road (six legs, ~100 min)

The body is **physically carried** — travois, or on his back. Not inventory. It
changes his walk, his run, his balance. **He must put Kelem down to fight, and
pick him up after.** Every encounter in the game is bracketed by that ritual: set
him down carefully, do something monstrous, come back, lift him, keep walking.

Late game, Kelem gets *lighter*. Which is worse.

At any moment, in any quiet stretch, the player can **simply walk away without
picking him up.** Never prompted. Never rewarded. Never acknowledged. If you do,
the game continues; Sarn reaches Nuhal empty-handed, the way in does not exist for
him, and you get nothing. The point is that the option was there in every quiet
moment of the entire game and you didn't take it either.

| Leg | Place | Kelem | Sarn | World detail (seeded) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anhad** — the salt flats | Wrapped, treated, dignified | Confident, talkative, funny | A funeral in the distance. Ordinary. |
| 2 | **The Mern crossing** | The smell starts; he buys herbs | Still charming, still explaining | The crossing is busy. Nobody is coming back the other way. |
| 3 | **Dresh** — more graves than houses | He has to hide the body from people | First kill of the game. Ugly, panicked, over nothing. | The village is generous and dying. Ithe joins. |
| 4 | **The Hanging Wood** | He stops looking at the face | Stops talking to Kelem. Starts talking to himself. | Bodies on ropes. Some rope is twenty years old. It is maintained. |
| 5 | **Oruk** — the drowned city, waist-deep | Barely a shape | Kills efficiently now. No hesitation. | Oruk drowned because the mountain moved. |
| 6 | **The ascent** | Bones and cloth. A bundle. | Silent. Almost beautiful. Unreachable. | Hundreds of other people are climbing. Carrying bodies. You were never alone; you just couldn't see the road from below. |

**Seeding rule:** one or two details per leg that mean nothing on a first pass and
are unbearable on a second. Never enough to solve it early. Never winked at.

### ACT III — The turn (~80% mark)

**The mouth of Nuhal.** He's made it. Twenty years. The player is *with him* —
this is the payoff.

A masked figure waits, alone, not attacking. She takes the mask off.

Her face is covered in the sickness. Vessa explains — calmly, tiredly, without
accusation — what the masks are, what the burned shrines were, what the hanged
bodies were, who those two hundred people were. She asks him once to turn around.

He kills her mid-sentence. Not in rage — in **dismissal**. A man twenty years into
this cannot afford one second of consideration.

Every fight the player has already played was that. There was nothing else under
it. They just had his voice over the top.

### ACT III — The silent replay (~10 min, no combat)

The journey again, compressed, **with the narration removed.** Same route, same
scenes, same footage. Just walking past what you already walked past:

- Dresh, which he called "already gone," had people in it. They watched an old
  man carry a corpse through their street.
- The Hanging Wood is a graveyard, tended.
- The figure who "ambushed" you at the Mern was standing in front of a well.
- **Ithe is talking the whole time.** In the background of every scene, trying to
  reach him, being talked over. You couldn't hear her before because he was
  louder. Now you can.

No commentary. Just the world without the man in front of it.

### ACT III — The stand (the drone)

The last of the Masked. Dozens of them, sick, half-dead, badly armed, throwing
themselves at one old man to stop him reaching the summit.

**Deliberately monotonous.** No music. No score cue, no swell, nothing — just
breath, impact, and the weather. It runs long. It is not fun and it is not
supposed to be. It is the Drakengard drone deployed once, at the exact moment it
does maximum damage.

You have to kill all of them to proceed.

### ACT III — The summit

The Guest does not fight. It is nearly awake and no longer needs to eat. It does
not notice him. It does not notice Kelem.

The confrontation is Sarn swinging at something that does not register the
impact, **for as long as the player is willing to keep pressing the button.** No
health bar. No phases. No end state.

*You* stop. Not him. He would go forever.

## 6. Endings

Sequential — each unlocked by replaying with what you now know. Each makes the
previous ones worse. There is no sixth.

**A — THE DESCENT.**
He is discarded. Set down at the base, alive. Credits. Players will think this is
the ending. The mountain's shadow is measurably longer than in the opening shot.

**B — THE VIGIL.**
He sits at the foot of Nuhal and does not leave. Years pass in a single held
shot. Pilgrims walk past him carrying their dead and he says nothing — he *can't*,
because the one sentence that might stop them is the sentence that makes Kelem's
death his. The sky changes behind him.

**C — THE CHARTS.**
He goes home and keeps working. Better maps than before — the best of his life,
because now he's walked every line. The final frame is a beautiful finished chart
of a road that should not exist. He is at peace. This is the warm ending and it is
the most obscene one.

**D — THE MERCY.**
He starts killing pilgrims on the road before they can reach the mountain. He
becomes, precisely, the thing he spent twenty years slaughtering. It works
locally. It is far too late. He never finds that out, and nobody will ever know
why he did it.

**E — THE SECOND MAN.** *(true ending)*
Playable, short, quiet: **Kelem's last eleven months**, from the inside.

He worked out what the survey had done. He met the Masked; they told him; he
believed them. He decided not to tell Sarn — because Sarn would not have heard it,
and because a man who has built his whole life on his own lines cannot be told
that the lines are the weapon.

His last request was to be burned.

Sarn promised. Knelt beside him. Then picked him up and started walking.

**Final shot: the opening shot of the game, from over Kelem's shoulder, receding.**

After E, every other ending retroactively gets worse. That's the structure.

## 7. Gameplay spec (minimum viable)

- **One heavy attack.** Long wind-up, long recovery, no cancel. Committed. No
  combos. Positioning and footing are the entire skill expression.
- **No dodge-roll.** A shoulder turn and a stumble-step. He is seventy-one.
- **No upgrades, no progression, no loot.** He never gets better at this. What
  changes across the game is that he stops hesitating — carried purely in
  animation timing and recovery frames, never in stats.
- **Encounter density:** 6–9 named set-piece fights across the whole game, each a
  story beat. Almost no trash combat. Pure traversal between.
- **The drone:** used exactly twice. Once mid-game when he snaps (Leg 5), once at
  the Stand.
- **Carry system:** the load changes locomotion, stamina, and camera. Put-down and
  pick-up are full, uninterruptible animations. Never skippable.
- **Audio:** sparse. Most of the game has no music at all. Score exists to be
  withheld — when it finally arrives it should feel like a mistake.

## 8. Open questions

- Does Sarn have a voice actor, or is his narration text-on-black between legs?
  (Recommend: full VO. His voice is the whole interface.)
- Is the Guest ever rendered, or only implied through environment displacement?
  (Recommend: never rendered. Scale shown by what it moves.)
- Do we ever show Sarn's face clearly before Act III?
- Ending order: is E gated behind all of A–D, or behind A + any two?
